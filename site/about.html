About

Peptide Commons is an independent reference for people researching peptides. We sell nothing, take nothing from vendors, and publish the dataset so that disagreeing with us is straightforward.

Why this exists

Search for almost any peptide and the first page of results is storefronts. Each one carries a paragraph of mechanism, a paragraph of benefits, a "99% purity" badge and a research-use-only disclaimer in the footer. The information is not exactly false — it is selected. Failed trials are omitted, adverse events are compressed into a sentence, and the difference between an approved drug and a compound with rodent data only is invisible in the layout.

That structure is not malice; it is what happens when the page that informs you is the page that sells to you. The fix is not better disclaimers on shops. It is a reference with no transaction attached.

How entries are graded

Every compound gets a tier before it gets a description, and the tier grades the evidence, not the compound. The four tiers are defined in the dataset and shown on every entry:

  • Tier 1 — approved by a major regulator for a specific indication on the strength of adequately powered randomised trials.
  • Tier 2 — registered human trials with published results, but no approval for the use the community is interested in.
  • Tier 3 — small human studies, foreign-market approvals with thin published data, or a deep animal literature without controlled human evidence.
  • Tier 4 — animal or in-vitro data only, or human data that failed to show benefit.

A tier is a claim about the state of the literature, so it moves when the literature moves. Tier change requests go through the community with citations attached and are decided in public.

Funding

What we do not take No affiliate revenue. No sponsored entries. No advertising. No vendor sample programmes. No paid placement in the library, ever, under any framing.

Running costs are static hosting and a domain, and they are covered by the maintainers. If that ever changes, the funding source will be named on this page before it takes effect, not after. Curators declare any affiliation with a vendor, lab, clinic or compounding pharmacy publicly; undisclosed affiliation ends participation permanently.

The practical test: nothing on this site links to anywhere you can buy a peptide, and no page ranks sellers. If you ever see one that does, treat it as evidence the site has been compromised and tell us.

The dataset is the site

Every compound entry is generated from a single plain file, data/compounds.json. It is served uncompiled and unobfuscated. You can read it, diff it between revisions, fork it, or use it to check whether our summary of an entry matches the underlying fields.

The whole site is static HTML with three small scripts and no dependencies. There is no analytics, no tag manager, no font CDN, no embedded video and no third-party request of any kind. Loading any page here contacts exactly one server.

Corrections

Corrections with a citation are actioned rather than debated. The process is deliberately boring:

  • Raise it with a source

    Point at the specific field or sentence and give a citation — a paper, a label, a regulatory document.

  • Two curators review

    One drafts the change, a second checks it against the source. Disagreements go to the community thread rather than being settled privately.

  • The revision number increments

    Every published change bumps the dataset revision, which appears on the library page and in the digest.

  • The change is logged, not silently applied

    Substantive corrections are recorded with what changed and who raised it. Nothing is quietly edited.

What we are not

Not clinicians, and not your clinicians. Not a vendor review site — deliberately. Not a marketplace. Not a source of protocols: the calculators do arithmetic on numbers you supply and will never suggest a dose. And not neutral about risk — where the evidence is bad and the harm record is real, as with melanotan II, the entry says so plainly instead of hedging.

We are also not comprehensive. Twenty-two compounds is a starting point chosen by what the community actually discusses, and the gaps are real. Suggestions for additions go through the same citation-first process as corrections.
