Community

Community peptide reporting has a survivorship problem: successes get written up and abandonments go quiet. The log format here asks for the stop reason first, because that is the field that makes everything after it interpretable.

The log template

A report that omits these fields cannot be compared with anyone else's, which is why the great majority of community peptide reporting is unusable in aggregate.

Required fields in a Peptide Commons protocol log
FieldWhy it is required
Stop reasonAsked first, before anything else. Logs that end without one are the reason community data skews positive.
Baseline periodWhat you measured, for how long, before changing anything. Two weeks minimum.
Single variableWhat changed, and confirmation that nothing else did. Concurrent diet or training changes must be declared.
Objective endpointAt least one measurement that is not a feeling: bloodwork, weight, a load, a timed test, an imaging result.
Reconstitution mathsVial strength, diluent volume, resulting concentration, syringe reading. Shows the dose you report is the dose you took.
Storage historyHow it was shipped and stored. A log without this cannot distinguish "did not work" from "was degraded".
Adverse effectsIncluding ones you attributed elsewhere. Attribution is the reader's job, not the reporter's.
Clinician awarenessYes or no. Not a judgement — it is context for interpreting everything else.

Ground rules

  • No sourcing. At all.

    No vendor names, no links, no prices, no discount codes, no "DM me". This is enforced by removal without warning, including in private messages. It is the rule that keeps this a reference rather than a marketplace, and everything else here depends on it.

  • Claims carry citations

    Assertions about what a compound does need a source, and "a study showed" is not one. Anecdote is welcome when it is labelled as anecdote — the problem is anecdote presented as evidence.

  • Null results are first-class posts

    A well-documented "nothing happened" is more valuable than ten enthusiastic partial logs, and it is promoted accordingly rather than buried.

  • No dosing prescriptions to individuals

    Describe what you did and what happened. Do not tell another person what to take. The difference is not pedantic — it is the line between a log and unlicensed practice.

  • Medical emergencies go to medical services

    Threads describing acute symptoms are locked with a pointer to urgent care. Nobody here can assess you, and the delay while people try is the actual harm.

  • Disclose every interest

    Affiliation with any vendor, lab, clinic or compounding pharmacy must be declared in your profile. Undisclosed affiliation is a permanent ban, and we do check.

  • Minors and vulnerable users

    Eighteen and over. Threads about use during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or in under-18s are redirected to clinical care without discussion.

  • Curators can be wrong

    Tier assignments are arguable and get argued. Bring evidence and the tier moves — the correction log is public and records who was right.

Moderation posture

Small, slow and opinionated. Curators are named on the about page, decisions are logged, and sourcing removals are not appealable.

What this isn't

Not a marketplace, not a review site, not a substitute for a clinician, and not a place to ask what you should take. Those questions get closed politely and immediately.
