Compound library

Every entry graded before it's described

The tier sits at the top of the page, not buried at the bottom. It tells you how much weight the rest of the entry can carry.

Tier 1 · Approved

Regulator-approved

Adequately powered randomised trials behind a specific indication. Semaglutide, tirzepatide, tesamorelin, bremelanotide.

Tier 2 · Clinical

Human trials, no approval

Published registered trials exist, but not for the use the community wants. Retatrutide, thymosin α1, elamipretide.

Tier 3 · Early

Thin human, deep animal

Plausible mechanism, unproven outcome. BPC-157, MOTS-c, epithalon, Semax, Selank sit here.

Tier 4 · Preclinical

Anecdote or negative data

Animal-only, or human data that failed. TB-500, melanotan II, AOD-9604, DSIP, KPV.

BPC-157, honestly

The most-discussed compound in the community, with the widest gap between enthusiasm and published human evidence. We say so on the page.

The TB-500 identity problem

Nearly all the research used full-length thymosin β4 at ~4963 Da. Nearly all the vials contain a 861 Da fragment. Those are not the same molecule.

When we say don't

Melanotan II carries active regulator warnings and a dermatology case literature. We keep the entry precisely so that record is easy to find.
Tools

Arithmetic, not protocols

Unit confusion — mg against mL against insulin-syringe units — causes more community incidents than any pharmacological property. These calculators do the conversion and show you the barrel.

  • Reconstitution calculator

    Vial strength plus diluent volume gives concentration, the volume for a target dose, the reading on a U-100 syringe, and how many full doses the vial holds.

  • Syringe visualiser

    Draws the fill against a 30, 50 or 100-unit barrel and turns red when the dose won't fit — the failure mode behind most ten-fold errors.

  • Half-life & accumulation

    Accumulation ratio for a repeated interval, time to steady state, and time to washout. Useful for understanding why weekly and daily compounds behave nothing alike.

  • Runs entirely in your browser

    Nothing you type is transmitted. There is no analytics call, no form endpoint and no third-party script on any page of this site.

The unit trap

A U-100 insulin syringe reads 100 units per millilitre no matter how big the barrel is. "20 units" on a 30-unit syringe and "20 units" on a 100-unit syringe are the same volume — but the same mark on differently sized barrels is not.

Reconstitute the same vial with twice the water and every mark on the syringe is now worth half as much. Recalculate for every new vial.
