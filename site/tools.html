Calculators

Unit confusion causes more community incidents than any pharmacological property. These do the arithmetic and draw the syringe. They do not suggest a dose — that number has to come from a label, a trial, or a clinician.

What these tools are Conversion arithmetic. They take numbers you supply and restate them in other units. They contain no dosing recommendations, no protocol library and no compound-specific defaults, deliberately.

Reconstitution

Enter the vial strength printed on the label, the volume of diluent you are adding, and the dose you are calculating for.

The total peptide mass in the vial, from the label or CoA — not the concentration.

Bacteriostatic water for multi-use vials; sterile water only if the whole vial is used at once.

1 mg = 1000 mcg. Getting this wrong by one decimal place is the classic ten-fold error.

All U-100 syringes read 100 units per mL. The barrel size changes the maximum, not the scale.

Equivalent to
—

IU is not a mass unit. It is defined per substance against a biological reference standard, so there is no general IU-to-mg conversion — and any site offering one for peptides is guessing.
